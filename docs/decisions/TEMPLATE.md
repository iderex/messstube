## What is being decided

The question, stated so that somebody who was not there can tell what would count
as an answer. If two decisions are settled together, say why they are one
decision rather than two.

## The decision

The answer, in the fewest words that are still unambiguous. Numbers are fixed
here rather than left to whoever writes the code. Where the decision has parts,
give them each a name so that a later record can overturn one part without
reopening the rest.

## The reasons

Why this answer and not another. A reason that rests on a measurement carries the
command that produced it, run against what the reader will have rather than
against a working copy. A reason that is a judgement is written as a judgement,
in words that say so.

## What it costs

What is worse because of this decision. Every decision has a cost; a record with
an empty cost section has not found it yet.

This section is not softened later. If the cost turns out to be larger than
estimated here, that goes in the record that supersedes this one, and this
sentence stays as it was written.

## What was rejected and why

The alternatives, including the strongest one, and the reason each was not taken.
The strongest alternative gets stated fairly before it is rejected, because a
record that only argues its own side has not made a decision.

## What would reverse it

The observation that would show this was wrong. Something somebody could go and
check, not a change of mind.

If nothing could reverse it, say that and say why, because a decision nothing can
falsify is either an axiom of the project or an error, and the reader deserves to
know which one is being claimed.
